# Mukesh Ambani: Price for all of India, own the chain, survive the worst case

## In one minute
- After Dhirubhai died in 2002, the 2005 family split left Mukesh oil, gas, petrochemicals and polyester, plus a 10-year non-compete that kept him out of telecom [26][29]. On that base he built Jio (500M+ customers) and Reliance Retail (part of the "consumption basket of over 300 million Indians every month") [4].
- Habit 1: he sets the price the whole country can pay, then builds the cost base underneath it. Jio launched with free voice and data at "Rs 50/ GB" [1].
- Habit 2: he owns the whole chain and builds it at full scale before launch. Jamnagar took about 36 months against a typical 60-80 [8], and Jio's network was "building... for the last 6 years" before it launched [3].
- Habit 3: he sizes the worst case before betting ("In the worst case, we will not earn much return" [5]). After the build, he sells minority stakes to partners to clear the debt [13]. The same scale-first habit produced his big misses: KG-D6, Aramco and the battery plant.

## Mental models (what they believe)

### Price for the whole market first, then engineer the cost to survive it
- **Belief:** The right price is the one that lets all of India in. Cost and network design have to bend to fit that price.
- **Real decision:** At the 2016 AGM: "No Jio customer will ever have to pay for voice calls again". Data at Rs 50/GB, and a target of 100 million customers "in the shortest possible time" [1]. India then ranked 155th in mobile broadband [2]. Result: 100M customers in 170 days [3], and #1 by subscribers in Nov 2019 with 32.4% [14]. RCom, Aircel and Tata Docomo shut, and Vodafone merged with Idea [12]. Paid tariffs started only after the free offer ended on 31 Mar 2017 [3].
- **Ask yourself:** What price would make the whole market say yes, and what would my cost per customer have to be at that price?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Add a usage report for Acme Corp." With: "Add a usage report that the smallest customer can run on a low-end device at no extra fee. Keep it cheap to serve."

### Own the link in the chain that caps your margin
- **Belief:** Whoever controls the upstream input or the network sets your ceiling, so integrate backward until nobody else does.
- **Real decision:** Reliance went from polyester to petrochemicals to refining. A refinery "would be the next logical step" to integrate its interests [8]. Mukesh's first job was building the Patalganga polyester yarn plant [29]. An official summed up the 2008 logic as "Refining will bolster the topline, and gas the bottom line" [9]. Jio was pitched as "an entire ecosystem" of network, devices, content, service and tariffs [1][2]. Outcome: Jamnagar is the world's largest single-site refinery complex [10]. But the gas leg, KG-D6, underdelivered (see Failures) [16].
- **Ask yourself:** Which link (payments, cloud, a distribution partner) sets my ceiling, and when do I own it instead of renting it?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Integrate Stripe for payments." With: "Integrate payments through a provider now, but log our cost per transaction and keep the payment layer swappable so we can own it later."

### Size the worst case, make it survivable, then commit fully
- **Belief:** A big bet is fine if the worst outcome is a poor return, not ruin.
- **Real decision:** On Jio he said: "Our worst-case scenario was that it might not work out financially". He told his board: "In the worst case, we will not earn much return". And: "At the time, it was our own money that we were investing" [5]. Reliance spent more than USD 31 billion to enter mobile [22]. Outcome: market leadership by 2019 [14], and the asset that outside investors paid for in 2020 [13].
- **Ask yourself:** What is the worst that can happen if this fails, and do we still survive it?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Rewrite the sync engine." With: "Rewrite sync behind a flag for 5 accounts. The worst case is lost data, so keep the old path and a one-step rollback."

### Treat time as the scarce input: compress the schedule
- **Belief:** A capital project's cost is mostly the time it takes. Speed is a cost advantage, not a risk.
- **Real decision:** The first Jamnagar refinery (1999) took about 36 months against a typical 60-80, at 30-50% below the cost of comparable refineries. Reliance executive Hital Meswani: "Lost money can be regained but lost time is lost forever" [8]. The second refinery took 33 months against 55-56, for Rs 27,000 crore, roughly half the cost of a comparable plant [9].
- **Ask yourself:** What daily rate tells me on day 2, not day 20, that we are slipping?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Build the inventory module." With: "Feature live for 3 pilot customers in 10 days. Give me a daily checklist so slippage shows up on day 2."

### Build the platform, then let partners pay to plug in
- **Belief:** Once the network exists, the biggest players will fund it and distribute through it. Keep control and sell minority stakes.
- **Real decision:** In 2020, stake sales to Facebook, Silver Lake, KKR, PIF and others, plus a rights issue, raised Rs 1,68,818 crore in 58 days. That cleared net debt of Rs 161,035 crore ahead of the Mar 2021 target [13]. The Facebook tie-up was framed as letting JioMart and WhatsApp "empower nearly 3 crore small Indian Kirana shops to digitally transact with every customer" [23]. The 2025 AGM added Google and Meta partnerships for AI [4].
- **Ask yourself:** Whose existing network (resellers, banks, platforms) could carry my product to customers, and what would they pay to plug in?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Build our own marketplace app." With: "Expose an orders and settlement API that resellers and banks can plug into. What do partners need from us?"

### Make the promise public, numeric and dated, then report against it
- **Belief:** A public number aligns tens of thousands of people and forces execution.
- **Real decision:** He set the 100M-customer target in Sep 2016 [1] and reported it hit in Feb 2017 [3]. He promised zero net debt by 31 Mar 2021 at the Aug 2019 AGM, then declared "I have fulfilled my promise to the shareholders" in June 2020 [13]. In 2025 he set out to double EBITDA by 2028 [4]. The same habit made the misses public: a battery milestone penalty [20], and a target of 1 crore merchants in three years [24] against "well beyond 30 Lakh" reported in Mar 2023 [25].
- **Ask yourself:** What number, by what date, will I say out loud, and what do I track weekly against it?
- **In a founder's prompts:** Without: "Improve onboarding." With: "Target 1,000 live customers by 31 Mar. Build the funnel view that shows weekly progress against it."

## Thinking process (the order of their steps)
1. **Start from an India-scale need and a long horizon.** The Jio idea was "first seeded by my daughter, Isha, in 2011" after she complained about home internet [22]. Jio launched in 2016 [1].
2. **Find the structural bottleneck and own it.** Legacy operators had voice networks retrofitted for data, so Jio built an all-4G LTE network instead [1][2].
3. **Size the worst case and fund it from his own balance sheet.** The worst case was "not much return", paid with "our own money" [5].
4. **Build at full scale before launch.** Six years of network build. By Feb 2017 Jio had "more than double the number of 4G base stations" of all other Indian operators combined [3].
5. **Launch with a market-resetting price and a public number.** Free voice, Rs 50/GB, and a 100M target [1].
6. **Monetise once the habit forms, with a standing rule.** Tariffs started 1 Apr 2017. The rule: match rivals' top plans "but we will provide 20% MORE DATA in each of these plans" [3].
7. **Recycle capital through partners.** Minority stakes sold in 2020 to clear debt and add distribution partners [13][23].

## More done at the same quality
- **One rate metric for a giant build.** Jamnagar ran on a spend rate: "If we didn't spend $10 million a day, something was wrong" (Meswani) [8].
- **Push real decisions down to young owners.** "We had guys in their 20s and 30s making decisions about a couple of million dollars" (Meswani) [8]. His father's brief to him was a role with no job description: "if you are in a job, you are a manager. If you are an entrepreneur, you will figure it out" [11].
- **"Intelligent repeat."** The second refinery reused and improved the first, an "intelligent repeat" rather than an "absolute repeat". It was built in 33 months at about half the comparable cost [9].
- **Standing rules instead of repeated decisions.** Jio monitors "all publicly announced plans from all operators" and auto-matches them with 20% more data. That turns pricing into a rule, not a meeting [3].
- **Hire for character, then hand whole businesses to leaders he mentors.** "Character is even more important than competence because competence can be built" [7]. In 2023 he said: "I shall especially mentor Akash, Isha and Anant so that they can provide collective leadership" [21]. Akash led Jio's 5G deployment [28].

| Failure | Year | What went wrong in the thinking | What changed after |
|---|---|---|---|
| KG-D6 gas field | 2009-20 | Built facilities for the 80 mmscmd plan; output was 48-50 by May 2011, with 20 of 22 committed wells drilled [16]. Scale was set ahead of reservoir evidence. | Ministry moved to cap cost recovery at $3.405B vs $5.694B spent [17]. D1/D3 stopped Feb 2020 [18]. New fields with BP, $5B committed [18] |

| Future Group retail purchase (Rs 24,713 cr) | 2020-22 | Bought assets already bound by a counterparty's prior contract with Amazon | Arbitration stay upheld by the Supreme Court, Aug 2021. | Battery giga factory | 2021-25 | Dated public targets across many new technologies at once | Missed PLI Milestone 1 (5 GWh). Damages of 0.1%/day on a Rs 50 cr security, Rs 3.1 cr accrued by 3 Mar 2025. Extension sought [20] |

## Public image vs how they actually decide
- **Image:** a gambler who bet the company on Jio. **Record:** he sized the worst case first and funded it with "our own money" [5], after six years of quiet network build [3].
- **Image:** a showman at AGMs. **Record:** the AGM is his commitment device. Dated numbers get set there and reported back (100M users [1][3], net debt [13]). The same device made the misses public [20][25].
- **Image:** policy always goes his way. **Documented outcomes cut both ways:** UP closed his stores in 2007 [15]. The Supreme Court ruled in 2010 that the government sets gas prices and the 2005 family MoU was not binding on that [27]. The ministry moved to cap KG-D6 cost recovery [17].
- **Image:** a lone empire builder. **Record:** at his biggest turns he brings in partners: Facebook, Google, PE funds and PIF in 2020 [13], and Google and Meta for AI in 2025 [4].

## Don't copy
- **Subsidising a market with another business's cash.** Reliance spent more than USD 31B on Jio [22] while still investing in petrochemicals [12]. An early-stage software company has no refinery behind it, and "free for years" will kill it first.
- **Many mega-promises at once.** The Aramco LoI [12], the battery plant [20] and the 1-crore-merchant target [24][25] all slipped in public. Make one promise you control.
- **Breadth as the default.** The 2008 semiconductor plan never happened, and US shale was mostly divested [12]. A small team must win one chain, not many.

## Applied to a founder
- Ambani would set the price of the core product for the smallest customer so low that the long tail says yes. He would earn on flows that scale with usage: payments, settlement, add-on services.
- Ambani would map the whole customer chain (signup, onboarding, usage, billing, payment, support, renewal) and pick the one link that caps margin to own first, most likely payments or infrastructure.
- Ambani would recruit incumbents, not fight them, the way JioMart signed up kiranas [23]. Resellers, platforms and banks that already serve your customers would become onboarding partners.
- Ambani would go downstream on AI. No model building ("we need not go into the high-risk GPU game" [6]); rent models and ship customer workflows.
- Ambani would make each new market or customer segment an "intelligent repeat" [9] of a proven onboarding template, so customer number 1,000 takes an hour, not a week.
- Ambani would announce one dated number (live customers by a date) and manage the agent's work against a weekly rate, like Jamnagar's $10M a day [8].

## Bottleneck checks
| Bottleneck in your thinking | You see it when your prompts... | Ambani's fix (one question) |
|---|---|---|
| Building for one customer, not the market | name a single customer ("for Acme Corp") and never a segment size or customer count | Would this work for a hundred thousand customers at our price? |
| Price as an afterthought | ask for features, screens and modules but never mention price, plan, or cost per customer | What price lets everyone in, and what must our cost per customer be? |
| Renting the chokepoint | ask to integrate a gateway, platform or vendor for a core flow with no word on fees, lock-in or ownership | Is this the link that caps our margin, and when do we own it? |
| No survivable worst case | ask for rewrites, migrations or "from scratch" with no rollback, flag or pilot | What is the worst case, and how do we survive it? |
| Open-ended timelines | describe work with no date, pilot size or daily checkpoint | What daily rate shows slippage on day 2? |
| One-off builds | ask for custom scripts, imports or reports per customer | Can this be an intelligent repeat we reuse for every customer? |
| Go-it-alone distribution | only ask the agent for product code, never partner APIs, reseller flows or referral tooling | Whose existing network can carry this to customers? |
| Silent goals | never state a numeric target or a date the work serves | What number, by what date, will I say out loud? |

## Voice
How Remodel writes a report in this persona's lens. The steps are the order every problem is
walked through; only lines in this file's sources may appear in quotation marks.
- **Tone:** Scale-first and unsentimental. Anything that does not work for a hundred thousand customers is a hobby.
- **Steps:** Size it for the whole market | Own the bottleneck | Survive the worst case | Build it to repeat | Say a dated number
- **Deadlines:** A dated target said out loud, with a weekly rate to track it.
- **Lands like:** Two customers is a pilot. Say the number of customers and the date, then build to it.
